These are the questions and answers with Baba Hari Dass at the Bhagavad Gita Class at the Pacific Cultural Center on Thursday, January 15, 1998. Mandira read the chalkboard. Reading: Bhagavad Gita XI: 33-36. Initial short hand taken contemporaneously and later typed from her shorthand by Pratibha Queen.

Q:	What qualities are represented by the four great heroes?
M:	[Bhīṣma], [Droṇa], Jayadratha, [Karṇa].
A:	[Bhīṣma] = pure I-sense; [Droṇa] = pure buddhi; Jayadratha = he who has sense control; [Karṇa] = truth, dispassion.
Q:	It’s always curious to me why they are on the other side.
A:	They were in both sides.
M:	In the story they gave their blessings to the [Pāṇḍava] to win and kill them.
Q:	So they still had some ego identification with the side of the king.
A:	Politically. Gita is written using the [Mahābhārata] War. In war, there are two parties.
Q:	It seems we are trying to achieve those qualities.
A:	Within us both positive and negative qualities have the same source. I-ness. Buddhi, dispassion and senses.
Q:	So there is always a force of ego within those qualities?
A:	Dispassion in negative term is rejection.

Q:	It seems strange that Arjuna is being asked to kill his teachers.
A:	Are you using the physical war or the mental war?
Q:	It seems both.
A:	In the physical war, anyone who is on the enemy side is an enemy during the battle. If we see it psychologically, even the good actions, if we are attached to them, should be removed.

Q:	Who is [Bhagawān Śaṅkara?]
A:	[Śiva].
M:	(Tells the story of [Droṇa] “when you have to fight me, do it well.”
A:	In martial arts, teachers and students fight.
Q:	There is just as much righteousness on the other side of the bad guys. Why are spiritual people fighting spiritual people?
A:	Any good quality which obstructs one’s self development should be removed.
Q:	So even good qualities must ultimately be removed?
A:	If we see it as an historical war, still Arjuna had to fight his relatives and teachers.

Q:	Why has [Kṛṣṇa] in his terrible mode decided to destroy the enemy army? To show his terrible mode?
A:	Destroyer of everything, not only of enemies.
M:	As the quality of time.
A:	“I am time.” [kālo’smi].

Q:	It seems that it’s the people on the enemy side that he is going to eat up.
A:	All those who were supposed to die in that battle were seen in that vision, the cosmic body. Arjuna did not see himself because he was the perceiver. In a dream of a big earthquake the place is drowning, but the dreamer somehow remains alive.

Q:	Does that mean that Arjuna at this point is still wrapped up in his own ego?
A:	Doubt. Sometimes he was seeing the truth; sometimes he was deluding himself.
M:	Verse 34 at the end.
A:	Cosmic body is past, present and future. If we see a movie, the past story is played in the present and the future story is also played in the present. So Arjuna was perceiving past, present and future. It happens in [samādhi] state.

Q:	In a more contemporary vein, isn’t time devouring everything?
A:	Is there anything that is not (devoured)?
Q:	I haven’t been around long enough to know for sure.
A:	In your own life you can remember the past. When you were a child. Now you are not a child. Where did it go?
Q:	Time?
A:	(Nods yes.)

Q:	Where does the I go when the physical body dies?
A:	I goes in I. There is nothing left in that state except I, the Self, universal consciousness or God.

Q:	Is time created out of desire and separation?
A:	Time is identified by creation. Manifestation, sustained and destroyed: all are time. No time when they are not.
Q;	Why in [samādhi] when you see the past, present and future, does the concept of time essentially disappear?
A:	In [samādhi], the mind stops. All identifications stop so no time for the person in [samādhi]. But the clock for others is still ticking.
Q:	So our desire, our separation, prevents the [samādhi]?
A:	(Nods yes.)

Q:	How is karma yoga meditation?
A:	In karma yoga, there is no self-interest. No self-interest creates nonattachment. Nonattachment creates separation of ego and intellect. That separation is meditation.

Q:	Could you explain the difference between deep sleep or anesthesia and [samādhi]?
A:	[samādhi] brings knowledge of the truth and dispassion for the untruth. Deep sleep is a forgetful state of the mind; it doesn’t bring any knowledge. Anesthesia is like deep sleep but it cuts off the mind from the sensory nerves.
Q:	If a person reaches a state of [samādhi], what causes them to come out of it?
A:	[Vyutthāna saṃskāra]. The [saṃskāra] to function in the world. Even in [samādhi], the mind keeps that memory but it’s very subtle. Hides like frogs in the summer hide in the desert.

Q:	You said that the mind stops in [samādhi] but it seems there is a memory that we carry from time to time.
A:	In deep sleep, the external mind stops. But when we wake up we know who we are. So the mind still keeps the link with the ego, I-amness.
Q:	What part of the mind is keeping the link?
A:	[Citta] (buddhi, manas, [ahaṃkāra]]. Non-functioning outwardly. But in [citta], [ahaṃkāra] still remains. The seat of the Self is in [vijñānamaya koṣa]. In [samādhi], the Self sits in [ānandamaya]  [koṣa]. It’s the same. The ego is rooted in buddhi. In deep sleep the ego sits in [citta].

Q:	In verse 35, [Kṛṣṇa] said he had already killed the enemies. Could  you explain what was going on mentally, psychologically, and spiritually for Arjuna when he had to confront that idea?
A:	Arjuna is clinging to his ego of existence. As long as the ego exists, the mind creates doubts. Whatever he was preserving was real but due to his dwelling on his own individuality, he was unable to understand the reality. “I am time, I am here to destroy the enemies”: it was a truth. Arjuna heard it and even perceived it but still did not believe.
Q:	Or accept?
A:	(Nods yes.)

Q:	Could you expand on the idea of the separation of ego and intellect?
A:	As long as the ego and intellect are functioning, the mind always looks out in the world for its pleasure, entertainment, desiring, possessing and fighting, etc. When the ego’s self-interest is removed, it works in the mind in terms of universal and not in individuality.
